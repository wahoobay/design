B, with a second colour. Which accent, and does a dark live page hold?

Four accents on the same Clear Water page. The blue stays the brand: logo, buttons, links, headline numbers. The accent gets one job: the things that are alive right now. The live badge, species chips, the second line on charts, and the Donate button, so it never competes with the blue for "click here". Each shade was adjusted until its text passes on white; the number in each card is the contrast ratio.

Below the four, the dark live-camera page in the same system, with a switch to try each accent on it. The teal you liked in C is the dark-page shade of option 1; on white it has to deepen to the shade shown, which is what you were sensing.

1. Seagrass teal

Sea-green rather than cyan, so it separates from the blue. #0B7F72, 4.9:1 on white; #2DD4BF on dark. Reads as water and nature; the closest relative of the blue, which is both its charm and the risk: at a glance some people see one cool family.

2. Coral

The reef's own warm colour and the blue's opposite, so nothing is ever confused for brand. #C94A27, 4.7:1 on white; #FF8A65 on dark. Strongest "live" and "donate" signal of the four. Risk: used too widely it starts to shout.

3. Mangrove green

Picks up the tree in the logo and says conservation without a word. #2F7A4B, 5.2:1 on white; #6DD39A on dark. Calm and credible next to the blue. Risk: blue-and-green is the eco default, so the rest of the page has to carry the character.

4. Sun amber

Sand and sunlight; the warmest and most "come outside" of the four. Amber only works as a fill with dark text (#F6BC45), never as coloured text, so small labels use a brown #8A5A00, 5.6:1. Risk: yellow reads as caution in data screens.
